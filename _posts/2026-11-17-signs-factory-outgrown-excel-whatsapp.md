In many growing factories, the shop floor runs on three things: an Excel sheet in the planning office, a WhatsApp group for the supervisors, and a paper register at each machine. For years, this works. The planner knows every job. The owner knows every customer. A photo of a job card on WhatsApp answers most questions.

Then the factory grows. More machines, more parts, more customers, a second shift, a few subcontractors. The same Excel sheet and WhatsApp group are still there, but now they are slowing the factory down instead of helping it.

This article lists the signs that your factory has outgrown Excel and WhatsApp, and what to do first. It is not a reason to throw away what works. Excel and WhatsApp did their job. The question is whether they can still carry the load.

## Why Excel and WhatsApp work at first

They are free or cheap, everyone already knows them, and they need no training. Excel is flexible: the planner can add a column in a minute. WhatsApp is fast: a supervisor can ask the stores in-charge about material and get an answer in seconds.

The trouble is that both depend on a few people. The Excel sheet is only as current as the last time the planner updated it. The WhatsApp group holds the answer, but only for those who read it at the time. When those people are on leave, busy or have left the company, the information goes with them.

## Eight signs your factory has outgrown Excel and WhatsApp

### 1. Nobody trusts the planning sheet without a phone call

If a supervisor reads the plan and then calls the planner to check whether it is still right, the sheet is no longer the source of truth. The phone call is.

### 2. There are several versions of the same file

"Plan final", "Plan final 2", "Plan updated Monday". When two people save copies, the factory has two plans. Someone is working from the wrong one.

### 3. The answer to "where is this job card?" is "let me check"

A customer calls about an order. To answer, someone walks the floor, asks the supervisor, scrolls WhatsApp, and checks the subcontracting register. If finding one job card takes more than a few minutes, the records are not keeping up. Our article on [why your ERP cannot tell you where a job card is]({{ '/blog/why-your-erp-cannot-see-your-shop-floor/' | relative_url }}) explains why this gap exists even in factories with an ERP.

### 4. Important decisions live in chat messages

"Run job 4512 on machine 3 after lunch." "Send 40 pieces to the heat treatment vendor today." These are real decisions, but they sit in a chat. A month later, nobody can find them, and there is no record of who decided what.

### 5. Month-end takes days of matching

If closing the month means matching the Excel sheet against paper registers, delivery challans and memory, the daily records are not complete. The numbers never quite agree, and the team spends days arguing about which one is right.

### 6. Stop reasons and rejections are guesses

When the production sheet is filled at the end of the shift, stops become "machine down" and rejections become a single number with no reason. You can see that output was low, but not why.

### 7. Only one or two people understand the sheet

Many factories have a planner whose Excel file has grown over years, with colours, hidden columns and formulas only they understand. If that person is away for a week, planning slows down or stops.

### 8. Customers ask questions you cannot answer quickly

Export customers and larger buyers often ask which material heat a lot came from, which machine made it, or when it was inspected. If answering means searching through files and registers, the records were not built for traceability.

If three or more of these signs are true in your factory, it has outgrown the tools. If five or more are true, the tools are costing you time every single day.

## What to do first

Moving away from Excel and WhatsApp does not need to happen all at once. A practical order:

1. **Write down the current flow.** Follow one job card from material issue to dispatch. Note every place information is written: Excel, WhatsApp, paper, a person's memory.
2. **Find the one question you cannot answer.** For most factories it is "where is this job and when will it be ready?" Start there.
3. **Choose one area to start.** One section, one shift, a few machines. Do not try to change the whole plant in one week.
4. **Record at the point of work.** Entries made at the machine, at the time, are more accurate than entries typed up at the end of the shift.
5. **Keep WhatsApp for talking, not for records.** A quick question is fine. A decision about a job, a quantity or a vendor should go into the record.
6. **Review after 30 days.** Check whether the question from step 2 is now easier to answer.

## A worked example

For example, take a sample machine shop (not a real company) with 14 machines, two shifts and two heat treatment vendors. The owner is asked to score the eight signs. The results look like this:

| Sign | True today? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan needs a phone call to confirm | Yes | Supervisors call the planner every morning |
| Several versions of the plan | Yes | Three files in the shared folder |
| Finding a job card takes a walk | Yes | Mostly jobs at the vendor |
| Decisions in chat | Yes | Vendor dispatch agreed on WhatsApp |
| Month-end matching takes days | Yes | Output and challans do not match |
| Stop reasons are guesses | Yes | Filled at shift end |
| Only one person understands the sheet | No | Two planners share it |
| Customer questions are slow | No | Few traceability requests so far |

Six signs out of eight are true. The owner picks the biggest pain, jobs lost at the vendor, and starts by recording every send and receive for the heat treatment stage, with a due date for each lot. After a month, the morning phone calls about vendor jobs reduce because the record answers the question.

These figures are sample data, shown only to explain the method.
